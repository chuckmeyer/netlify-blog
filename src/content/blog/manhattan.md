## The King of Cocktails

Few drinks carry the weight of history that the Manhattan does. Born in New York City sometime in the 1880s, it predates Prohibition, survived it, and emerged on the other side as one of the most beloved cocktails in the world.

The formula is deceptively simple: whiskey, vermouth, bitters. But the ratios and the quality of each ingredient make all the difference between a forgettable drink and something transcendent.

## Choosing Your Rye

The traditional Manhattan calls for rye, and for good reason. Rye whiskey's spice and pepper notes push back against the sweetness of vermouth in a way that bourbon can't quite manage. That said, bourbon Manhattans have their fans — the sweeter, fuller body creates a different but valid cocktail.

For rye, look for something in the 100-proof range. Rittenhouse is the classic bar choice. Sazerac 6-Year and Pikesville are worth the premium.

## The Vermouth Question

Vermouth is a living ingredient. Once opened, it oxidizes. Treat it like wine: store it in the fridge and use it within a month. A Manhattan made with stale vermouth sitting at room temperature for six months is not a Manhattan worth drinking.

Carpano Antica Formula is the gold standard — rich, complex, almost chocolatey. Punt e Mes adds more bitterness. For a cleaner, more classic profile, Dolin Rouge is reliable and affordable.

## Stirred, Not Shaken

This is not negotiable. Shaking dilutes the drink unevenly, aerates it, and clouds it. A Manhattan should be crystal clear, silky, and cold. Stir with conviction — 30 full seconds over ice — then strain into a pre-chilled glass.

The chill matters. A room-temperature coupe will bring the drink up to a disappointing temperature within minutes.
